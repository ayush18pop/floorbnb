## What the director changed after seeing the proof
1. PACE: much slower. 90 BPM, beat = 20 frames. Holds of at least 1.3 s on every line of big type, at least 5 s on every real screen shot. Fewer cuts: scenes are joined by camera moves, not hard cuts (cuts only at the two places SCRIPT.md names).
2. FLOW: the video follows the real flow of the app: landing page, then the simulator, then the app builder, review, confirmed, position, keeper, then agents.
3. TRUE 3D CAMERA: the whole film is one continuous camera path through a paper diorama in 3D space. Type cards and real screen panels are flat paper planes at different depths and orientations. The camera dollies, orbits a few degrees, tilts and flies from plane to plane, so the perspective of the screens visibly changes in 3D (foreshortening, parallax between layers, a panel swinging from an angled pose to face-on as the camera arrives). Implement a real pinhole camera (position, look-at, focal length) and project each plane with a homography (OpenCV `warpPerspective`, pip `opencv-python-headless`, with Lanczos or area filtering so UI text stays crisp when the plane is near face-on). Face-on arrival poses must show UI text sharp at 100 %. Angled poses may soften slightly: avoid holding a readable screen at more than about 25 degrees of yaw for more than 1 s. No depth of field, no heavy motion blur, no glass, no gradients (BRIEF.md stands).
4. SMOOTHER: motion is continuous at 30 fps with smooth eased curves (ease-in-out cubic or a gentle expo-out). No quantized or stepped camera or panel motion. The printed feel now lives in the INK layers only: grain, misregistration drift and halftone shimmer update at 15 fps (every 2nd frame, held), word-group type reveals stay stepped on beats. Not 60 fps; 30 fps output.
5. TYPOGRAPHY is a first-class deliverable: see SCRIPT.md. Big type cards, callout stickers next to the real UI, mono captions. Exact words only as written in SCRIPT.md. Typeset with care: kerning, optical alignment, line breaks as written, no orphan words, grid placement.

## Addendum (director, after the user's second note): CAMERA BLUR and SOUND TEAM
9. CAMERA BLUR IS REQUIRED (this reverses the v1 "no depth of field" rule). The user wants to SEE the lens work: text and planes blur and sharpen as the camera moves forward and backward.
   - Depth of field: thin-lens style. Each plane gets a blur radius from its distance to the focal plane (circle of confusion from 1/z difference and a lens aperture parameter). Plain defocus blur (gaussian or disc, no bokeh highlights, no glow, no gradients added). Blurred ink and paper still only blend palette colors.
   - Rack focus: on every scene arrival the incoming plane (type card or real screen) starts defocused (about 12 to 18 px radius at 1080p), eases to exactly 0 over about 12 to 16 frames as the camera settles, while the plane being left goes soft and drifts out. Planes in front of and behind the subject stay softly defocused (3 to 8 px) during holds so depth reads. Big headline type visibly goes soft to sharp on arrival.
   - Motion blur: while the camera dollies or orbits fast, add motion blur with a 180 degree shutter by averaging 4 to 6 sub-frame projections (homography at sub-frame times). Dolly gives radial/zoom smear, orbits give lateral smear. Only during moves. Max blur length about 24 px.
   - READABILITY RULE: a plane that is the subject of a hold (real UI shot or headline card) is exactly sharp (blur radius 0, no motion blur) for its whole hold, at least from 14 frames after arrival to 8 frames before departure. Real UI pixels are never tinted, halftoned or color changed; blur is only a lens effect while the camera is moving or the plane is out of focus.
   - Cost: allow up to 45 minutes for the master render; blur only on frames that need it; cache static layers. Report time per frame with and without blur.
   - QA: `contact/blur_study.png` showing a rack focus on a headline card (6 frames) and a dolly on a real screen (6 frames); report blur radius per scene and the frame ranges where each subject is exactly sharp.
